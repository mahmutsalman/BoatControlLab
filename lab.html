⛵ Three captains and YOU, one order: “turn from 0° to 90°”

Same boat, same rudder (dümen) limit. Every 0.1 s each captain decides the rudder. You steer the white boat with ← → (hold), space = rudder to 0.

🚤 The boat (the MODEL): rudder 10° → the boat WANTS to turn Kb × 10 = 2°/s (Kb = 0.2). It doesn't get there at once: after T = 3 s it has reached about 2/3 of it. In code, every 0.1 s: turn += 0.1 × ((Kb × rudder − turn) / T + wind) · heading += 0.1 × turn

your hands on the rudder. Hold ← → . Below: what the formulas would do in YOUR spot right now.

🧑‍✈️ PID

    
looks only at the ERROR. Knows nothing about the boat.

    
    

    

    
    
  • ✅ no model needed · 3 multiplications · the I part kills a steady push (wind)
  • 🔴 doesn't know the limit: the sum keeps growing while the rudder is stuck at 35° (“windup”) → big overshoot

    
every variable

      
errortarget − heading, in °. How far the bow still has to swing. Positive = turn right more.

      
P = Kp × errorpush NOW, in proportion to how far away you are. Big Kp = strong pull.

      
sumerror added up every 0.1 s (unit °·s). The MEMORY: it grows while you stay short of the target.

      
I = Ki × sumpush from error that STAYS. This is what beats a steady wind. Also what overshoots (windup).

      
turnhow fast the bow swings now, °/s. The error shrinks exactly that fast, so “change of error” = −turn.

      
D = Kd × (−turn)the BRAKE: the faster you already swing toward the target, the more it pulls the rudder back.

      
wants → rudderP + I + D can be any number; the real rudder stops at ±limit (“saturation”).

    

  

  

    
📐 LQR

    
knows the boat's equations. Computed its 2 gains ONCE, before the trip.

    
    

    
  • ✅ as cheap as PID while sailing · the gains come from the model + what you say you hate (qe, qr, R)
  • 🔴 rules (limit, 4°/s) are not in its math · no sum, so a steady wind leaves an error · wrong model → wrong gains

    
every variable

      
statethe 2 numbers that describe the boat right now: error and turn. LQR needs BOTH (PID's P and D used the same two).

      
kErrlike Kp: rudder degrees per 1° of error. Not hand-tuned: COMPUTED.

      
kTurnlike Kd: rudder degrees taken back per 1°/s of turning. Also computed.

      
qe, qr, Rthe score it minimises over the WHOLE future: qe·error² + qr·turn² + R·rudder², added up. You tune these 3, not the gains.

      
Riccatithe repeated calculation that finds kErr and kTurn from the model + qe, qr, R. It runs until the numbers stop changing, once, before the trip.

      
Q (the “Q” in LQR)Linear model · Quadratic score (the ²) · Regulator (holds a target).

    

  

  

    
🔮 MPC

    
has the model in its head and IMAGINES the next 8 s for many rudder plans.

    
    

    
  • ✅ rules are part of the choice (rudder ±limit, “never turn faster than 4°/s”) · sees what is coming
  • 🔴 heavy: ~100 000 imagined boat steps every 0.5 s · only as good as its model (unknown wind → error)

    
every variable

      
plan3 rudder values: A for 1 s, then B for 2 s, then C for 5 s = 8 s ahead (the “horizon”).

      
levelseach of A, B, C is one of 11 rudder values from −limit to +limit → 11³ = 1331 plans. The limit is built in: no plan can ask for 40°.

      
imaginerun the MODEL's boat equations 80 steps (8 s) for each plan. That is the “Model Predictive” part.

      
scorethe same as LQR's: Σ (qe·error² + qr·turn² + R·rudder²) × 0.1 s, plus a big penalty if the boat is not calm at the end.

      
rulea plan whose imagined turn goes past 4°/s is thrown out (red lines). A rule on the STATE: PID and LQR have no place for it.

      
first step onlyuse A for just 0.5 s, then imagine everything again from where the boat really is (“receding horizon”). This fixes small model errors.
